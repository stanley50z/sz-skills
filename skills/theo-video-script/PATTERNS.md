## Topic selection

A topic clears four tests.

1. **Fresh trigger.** Something happened in the last few days: a release, an announcement, a blog post, an incident, a fight between two companies. Release videos are filmed the same night ("I'm filming this at 2:00 in the morning").
2. **First-hand evidence.** He has used it, built with it, paid for it, or been hurt by it. Videos open with that proof: "$330,000 of inference over the last few weeks", "I installed a bunch of the skills", "I've been building more React Native than ever".
3. **A decision the viewer faces.** Which subscription to buy, which model to use for which job, whether to change how they work.
4. **A turn.** The stance differs from what the viewer expects or from what he said before: "I honestly kind of dismissed [it] initially", "prompt engineering... that's all crap", "I was wrong".

Topics he skips confirm the tests: "You might have noticed I didn't do a video on those models. There's a reason. They weren't that good."

## Titles

Two to seven words. The description, not the title, carries the facts. Recurring shapes:

- Withheld subject: "It's Here.", "Well This Was Unexpected...", "My new favorite model."
- Accusation: "You're using AI agents wrong", "Your prompts kinda suck"
- Direct address: "If you have a Claude sub, watch this"
- Flat verdict: "Jev is incredible", "Ox Alpha is INSANE"
- Stake or loss: "NVIDIA Just Lost Their Lead", "T3 Code broke me"
- Question: "Did AI Kill React Native?"
- Parenthetical twist: "(yes really)", "(Until Today)"
- Trailing ellipsis that hints at the verdict: "So I tried Matt's skills...", "So I've been testing Gemini 4..."

Titles overclaim and the video hedges. When the title baits, the first two minutes say so: "Okay, I may have baited you guys a bit", "I actually feel a little bit bad about how I'm starting this video because it's almost misleading".

## Skeleton

| Part | Share of runtime | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 3 to 8% (1:00 to 2:30) | Subject, one hard number, stance, personal stake, promise |
| Lead-in | 1 to 5% | Scope, disclosure, roadmap, or pre-empted objections |
| Body | 75 to 85% | Blocks of 1 to 6 minutes alternating source, show, zoom out |
| Honesty block | about 10%, placed at 75 to 90% | Weaknesses, failed runs, caveats, late disclosures |
| Verdict and close | 4 to 10% (1 to 4 min) | Placement verdict, emotional lift, question, sign-off |

The three body block kinds:

- **Source.** Read a post, article, chart or tweet aloud in chunks of one to three sentences and react after each.
- **Show.** His own demo, numbers, repo, dashboard.
- **Zoom out.** A framework, a story, an analogy, a rant.

## Body order by type

**Release reaction.** His own usage first, then price and availability, official claims, benchmarks in three layers (official, third-party, his own), novel strengths, a late visual set piece at 75 to 85% (his recurring "fish slop" game), real work in his own repo, weaknesses, verdict as a role in his workflow. Source reading is 15 to 55% of runtime.

**Comparison.** A visible category board. Categories run from least to most important to the audience, each closed with a one-line winner. Cost and subscription advice sit last. One long failure story is the emotional peak. Verdict split by viewer type.

**News commentary.** News stated in the first 10 to 60 seconds, then disclosures, the primary statements read aloud, the detail everyone missed, a critique of both sides, practical advice, a short emotional close. Explainer-first when the audience lacks background ("Before we can talk about how Nvidia loses, it's important to understand how they won").

**Source walkthrough.** The source's own headings are the outline. Read, restate in plain words ("To try and dumb this down"), apply it to himself, return ("Back to what Anthropic was saying here"). Source reading is 45 to 70%.

**Workflow tutorial.** A spoken roadmap of four or five parts, then live screen work on real tasks. Prompts are read aloud and annotated phrase by phrase. Principles get named as slogans near the end ("Safety nets, not guardrails"). Almost no outside sources.

**Experiment log.** Chronological. Each round raises the target, with a false ending in the middle ("We're done now, right? ... Not quite."), a cost reveal, the live payoff, then a second smaller case that generalizes the lesson.

**Opinion essay.** A reversal in the first 30 seconds, a concrete request answered live, his own mistake, a framework or taxonomy, a "spicy take" aimed at his own audience, an identity close. Demo prompts are fired early and their results harvested minutes later, so waiting time is filled with argument.

**Product tour.** Origin story with a confession, three pillars each with a demo, a rapid-fire feature list, a mock "negatives" section, positioning, credits.

## Hooks

Every hook names the subject within 30 seconds, gives one hard number or concrete surprise, states his stance, places him in the story, and withholds the why, the method or the verdict. Mechanisms seen:

- **Confession.** "I have a confession to make. Ever since I started focusing more on running my company and running my YouTube channel, I have been less good of an engineer." Then the contradiction: a chart of 50 merged PRs a week.
- **Diagnostic question.** "How many agent threads do you have running right now? ... If the answer is less than five, you really, really need to watch this video."
- **Quiz.** "How long do you think it has been since we got a normal-sized large Gemini model release?" Answer: eight months.
- **Metaphor plus raw news.** "Mom and dad are fighting and I'm going to be real with you guys, it's kind of stressing me out." Then the announcement read off the screen.
- **Rhythmic list.** "PHP? Pwned. Node JS? Pwned. ... And even Railway got railed."
- **History arc.** "Approximately 10 months ago, the way that we wrote code with AI changed fundamentally with a single model release." Rise, fall, then the new release against that bar.
- **Past self was wrong.** "Just 2 years ago, a blond guy with a mustache that somehow had access to this YouTube channel did a video called AI isn't going to keep improving."
- **Critic's words.** He reads a hostile tweet aloud, concedes it, and answers with a joke.
- **Genre break.** "Oh boy, it's new model time. This one's very different though."
- **Mystery with a number that doesn't add up.** "We have capacity for 100 trillion tokens per day." The identity is revealed inside the hook and a smaller secret is held for later.
- **Fake-out joke.** He names two irrelevant models as the big news, then "Obviously, I'm joking."

The hook ends on a promise ("I can't wait to show you ...") or a roadmap sentence listing what the video covers.

## Lead-in

The lead-in opens on a hard restart with no pause: "Let's dive into ...", "So, let's start with the official post", "Quick breakdown on how this all happened". It then does one or more of these jobs before the first body block:

- Scope: "I could probably go off for five plus hours about this model."
- Disclosure of money, access and bias: "I was an early investor in Cursor... I might make some money off this deal", "I do have early access, but I'm not being paid in any way, shape, or form. OpenAI has no influence over what and how I say things, just when."
- Roadmap: "I'm going to start by covering exactly what is changing ... But most importantly, I want to save some time at the very end to cover how to work around this."
- Pre-empted objections: "I can already tell this video is going to cause huge blowback on Twitter. So, I'm going to just list the questions they're all going to bring up."

## Argument moves

- **Reframe the axis.** He brings a two-sided model and judges the subject on it: System 1 vs System 2, intelligence vs behavior, spiky vs steady, human-scale vs agent-scale.
- **Official claim, then his own number.** A price cut against a 4x token gap. "100% in our evals" against 24 of 36 false claims in his audit.
- **The hidden variable.** One "the number you are looking at is the wrong number" moment per video: a 5% share measured in tokens, cache reads being 3% of his spend.
- **Concede, then hit.** Real strengths granted first ("There is some secret sauce in this model"), the damage after.
- **His own mistake as the lesson.** The dashboard he over-built, the prompt he got wrong on camera ("That is my bad"), the take he reverses mid-video ("Okay, this one flipped me").
- **Pre-empt the comment.** "And to those saying, 'Oh, it got lost in compaction or something.' No, it didn't." "Notice how I didn't say that."
- **Eliminate the audience's guesses** before giving his answer: scammers, crypto, then "Say it with me, chat. Distillation."
- **A friendly opponent as witness.** The known defender of the other side concedes the point.
- **Verdict as placement.** Which job the thing gets in his workflow, split by viewer type, tied to a per-task price: "use this like an if statement", "I will be defaulting to Fable for code and I'll be defaulting to Astra for every other thing".

## Evidence

- **Read and react.** One to three sentences read aloud, then a reaction of one to three short sentences ("Yep, that part I absolutely agree with", "This feels like it was written in 2024") or a plain restatement ("What this is trying to say is ...").
- **Numbers walked aloud as arithmetic.** "If anthropic models are three times less token efficient, then the 5% should be thought of more like 15%."
- **Charts get a skepticism pass.** Log versus linear scale, methodology caveats, anomalies named.
- **Counting as ridicule.** 21 unnecessary subtitles, the word "little" 15 times, nine em dashes on a page.
- **A fixed set of personal tests** reused across videos and cited as known quantities, flaws disclosed.
- **Own repo and own bill as the deciding evidence.** "I have not merged a thing that this model has shipped."
- **People credited by name** with a one-line bio. Insider claims hedged: "my understanding", "allegedly".
- **Everyday analogies** for the mixed audience: a fridge a mile from the kitchen, a manager handing out problems, an email inbox.

## Demo

- State the test and the failure mode being watched before running it: "This is the type of thing that throws a lot of models off."
- Real tasks on real projects, with a guess at how long it will take.
- Fire the prompt, keep talking, return when it finishes: "This one finished though, so let's take a quick look."
- Failures stay in and become the lesson. When a live run stalls, switch to a recorded run and come back later.
- Before and after read aloud side by side.
- A visual set piece late in the video.

## Retention

- **Open loops with a payoff point.** "Make sure I mention webhooks later. I promise you want to hear that part." "Remember those 40 sub agents, though, because we'll be talking about that in the agent section." Payoffs land 3 to 45 minutes later.
- **Visible structure** in long videos: a spoken roadmap, a category board, pace cues ("Let's blast through the rest of these").
- **One long story** with escalation and a twist in the second half of any video over 40 minutes.
- **Labeled rants** with an apology that resets the tone: "Okay, sorry. Just had to get that out of me."
- **Viewer participation.** Quizzes ("I'll give you a hint"), instructions ("open it and go to settings"), predictions.
- **Short verdict line** closing each segment, so a viewer who drifted gets a takeaway every few minutes.
- **Running gag** or refrain ("If you guessed burning more tokens, you're starting to catch on").
- **Future-video tease** tied to the subscribe ask.

## Endings

One to four minutes. A verdict or rule in one sentence, then an emotional or identity lift ("Never felt more like an engineer"), a question to the comments ("Am I crazy for saying all of this or am I kind of onto something?"), often a concrete next action he will take, and the sign-off. The subscribe ask sits mid-video next to a tease, rarely at the end.

## Voice

- Spoken second person: "you guys", "y'all".
- Long run-on sentences joined with "and", "but", "so", cut by resets of two to four words: "Not quite.", "Truly insane.", "It is what it is."
- Candor markers before an opinion or admission: "I'm going to be real with you guys", "I'll be real".
- Hype always carries a number.
- One-clause definitions for newcomers: "For those who don't know, ..."
- Signposts: "Let's start with ...", "Anyways, back to ...", "Okay, you guys get the idea.", "And here is where ..."
- Self-corrections stay in: "Actually, no, that's a lie."
- Mild insults softened right after: "And don't worry, you're not the only one."

## What stays his

His sign-off ("until next time, peace nerds"), his cast of friends, his running characters and grudges, his named benchmarks and his products belong to him. A script for the user builds the user's own versions of these: their own sign-off, their own repeatable tests, their own recurring bits.
